Open Porthole

Your tailnet, from any browser tab.

Porthole turns a browser tab into a temporary device on your Tailscale network. Open your private dashboards and admin pages on a computer where you can't install Tailscale, with nothing to set up and no server in between.

Open Porthole See how it works

Tested in current Chrome and Firefox, and in Safari on iPhone. See supported browsers and devices. An independent project, not affiliated with Tailscale.

How it works

  1. Open Porthole

    The tab downloads a copy of the Tailscale client built to run inside a browser. Nothing is installed on the computer.

  2. Sign in with Tailscale

    You approve the tab the same way you would approve any new device. It joins your tailnet as a temporary device, under your account and your access rules.

  3. Browse

    Pick one of your devices or type an address. The page opens inside Porthole, loaded straight from your tailnet.

What it's for

Porthole shows each site on an address of its own instead of its real one. Many self-hosted apps run fine that way, and a few features can't.

Works well

  • Dashboards, admin pages and the internal tools you built yourself, from monitoring and analytics to your NAS or router.
  • Username and password logins to your own apps, and the sessions that follow.
  • Pages that update live over WebSockets.
  • HTTP and HTTPS services on any port, by device name or by address, including ones with Tailscale HTTPS certificates.

Doesn't work

  • Sign in with Google, other third-party sign-in, and passkeys. They are tied to a site's real address.
  • SSH, file shares, remote desktop and game streaming. Anything other than a web page needs the Tailscale app.
  • Video calls and other apps that connect browsers directly to each other.
  • Fast video or large downloads. Traffic goes through Tailscale's relay servers, which suits pages better than big files.

Privacy and security

Porthole is a set of files your browser downloads. After that, the connection is between the tab and your tailnet.

No server in the middle

Pages travel encrypted from your devices to the tab. Porthole has no server that could read or store them.

Keys stay in the tab

Unless you choose to stay signed in, the tab's device keys are kept for the browser session only. Close the tab and they are gone.

A temporary device

The tab registers as an ephemeral device, the kind Tailscale removes by itself after it goes offline.

Staying signed in is your choice

Tick "Stay signed in on this browser" and the keys, along with site logins that are meant to last, are stored in that browser until you disconnect. The browser then shows up as a lasting device, which you can remove in the Tailscale admin console.

Your access rules apply

The tab can reach what your account is allowed to reach on your tailnet, and nothing more.

Sites are kept apart

Each site you open gets its own address, so one site can't read another's cookies or data.

What Tailscale sees

The same as for any device: the built-in client talks to Tailscale's coordination servers and sends its usual diagnostic logs.

Use Porthole on computers you trust. While the tab is open it has live access to your tailnet, and anything you type passes through that computer, so disconnect when you finish on a shared machine, and leave "Stay signed in" off there.

Questions

Do I need a Tailscale account?

Yes. Porthole connects to a tailnet you already have, for the times you're on a computer without Tailscale installed. Keep using the Tailscale app on your own devices.

Is Porthole made by Tailscale?

No. It's an independent project that builds on Tailscale's open-source client. Tailscale is a trademark of Tailscale Inc.

Which browsers does it work in?

It is tested in current Chrome and Firefox and in Safari on iPhone, and needs Chrome or Edge 84, Firefox 90, or Safari 16 at the least. Many TVs from 2022 onward have a new enough browser, while older TVs and most consoles do not. The compatibility page lists each one.

Why doesn't "Sign in with Google" work on my app?

Google only allows sign-in from the addresses registered for an app, and the browser reports the address Porthole shows the app on, not its real one. No page running in a tab can change that. Apps with their own username and password are unaffected.

Is it as fast as the Tailscale app?

No. A browser tab can't make the direct connections the app can, so traffic always goes through Tailscale's relays. Dashboards and admin pages feel normal, while video and large downloads are slow.

What happens when I close the tab?

The connection ends. Normally the tab's keys are discarded too, and you sign in again next time. If you ticked "Stay signed in on this browser", the keys stay in that browser and the next tab connects without asking. Your logins to the tailnet sites themselves follow the same rule: with "Stay signed in" on, a site that offers "remember me" will remember you in the next tab, and otherwise those logins end with the tab.

Can I have two Porthole tabs open?

One tab browses at a time. Opening Porthole in a second tab pauses the first, which offers a button to take over again.

Can I bookmark a page on my tailnet?

Yes. Porthole keeps the address you are viewing in its own address, after the #. Bookmark the tab and the bookmark reopens that page, asking you to sign in first if the tab is not connected. Browsers do not send that part of an address to any server.

I opened a device and nothing loaded. Why?

Choosing a device opens its default web address. If your service listens on another port or uses HTTPS, type that in the address bar, for example nas:8080 or https://nas.
